How the fire agents work

  1. An ignition creates four fire agents facing north, east, south and west. Each starts with a rate of spread equal to the flammability of its cell.
  2. Fire–atmosphere feedback: around the agents, the local wind mixes the global wind with the fire's own draught and is smoothed over neighbouring cells.
  3. Each agent updates its rate of spread and heading from flammability, wind, slope and how many other agents are close by.
  4. It moves; with spotting on, it can jump ahead and start a new fire.
  5. It pre-heats the cell ahead, which becomes more flammable.
  6. Agents in burnt-out or non-fuel cells (river, road) die.
  7. An agent that has travelled far enough into a new cell, with fewer than 3 agents there, splits into three (−45°, 0°, +45°).
  8. Agents consume fuel. The shape of the fire front emerges from all agents together.

Why this is agent-based (Unit 3 definition)

Individual objects that are created and destroyedFire agents are born (ignition, splitting, spotting) and die✓
Own state plus decision rulesEach agent keeps its rate of spread, heading and local density and updates them itself✓
Local interaction between agentsAgents react to nearby agents (density) and jointly shape the local wind✓
Environment the agents interact withThey read vegetation, wind and slope, and change it (pre-heating, fuel use)✓
Space and synchronous timeContinuous positions on a 96 × 64 landscape; 1 tick = 1 minute✓
Emergent global behaviourThe elongated, wind-driven fire front is not programmed; it emerges✓
Agents remember and learnNo memory or learning; fire is a physical process, not a decision-maker✗
